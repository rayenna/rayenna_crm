Between **June and September**, Kerala’s cloud cover and shorter sun hours reduce daily generation. This is expected — your system is still working. Focus on **annual** performance in **Track**, not a single rainy week.

## Why output drops

- Dense cloud cover blocks direct irradiance
- Shorter daylight hours
- Humidity and occasional panel film (dust + rain residue)

Annual energy models already account for monsoon seasonality.

## Practical tips

1. **Do not panic** — Compare year-to-date in Track to the same period last year (once you have history).
2. **Safe cleaning** — Avoid climbing on wet roofs. Rayenna schedules professional panel cleaning per your **Maintain** plan.
3. **Load timing** — On brief sunny breaks, run pumps or laundry when generation spikes.
4. **Tree growth** — Monsoon growth can add shading; trim trees near arrays when safe, or report new shading via **Maintain → Report an issue**.
5. **Inverter checks** — If generation is **zero** for multiple clear days, that is not normal monsoon behavior — report it.

## Battery-less systems

Most Rayenna rooftop systems are **grid-tied** without batteries. During a grid outage, the inverter shuts off for safety (anti-islanding). Generation resumes when grid power returns.

## When to contact Support

- Zero generation for 48+ hours on visibly sunny days
- Repeated inverter fault codes
- Physical damage from storms or falling branches

For urgent safety issues, use **Support → Emergency** call button.
