Once **net metering** is active, your KSEB bill reflects both power you draw from the grid and solar you export. Solar Hub **Track** shows generation and estimated savings; your **official bill** is the source of truth for credits.

## Key terms

- **Import** — Units you consumed from the grid (including at night or on cloudy days).
- **Export** — Excess solar units sent to the grid when generation exceeds on-site use.
- **Net units** — Import minus export for the billing period (rules depend on your tariff category).

## How solar reduces your bill

1. **Self-consumption** — Solar used in your home never appears as imported units.
2. **Export credit** — Surplus units exported earn credit per applicable KSEB rules.
3. **Fixed charges** — Connection charges and minimum tariffs may still apply even with solar.

## Track vs your KSEB bill

| Solar Hub Track | KSEB bill |
|-----------------|-----------|
| Daily / monthly generation from monitoring | Official meter readings |
| Estimated savings | Actual billed amounts |
| Useful for trends and alerts | Legal record for disputes |

Small differences are normal due to timing, meter vs inverter data, and billing cycle cut-off dates.

## Tips for reading your first solar bills

- Compare **three months before** and **after** net meter activation.
- Check that the meter type shows **bi-directional** or net metering on the bill header.
- If export always reads zero on sunny months, contact Support — the meter may not be configured yet.

## Maximizing bill savings

- Shift heavy loads (pump, washing machine, water heater) to **10 AM–3 PM** when possible.
- See **Monsoon solar tips** if output drops seasonally — annual totals matter more than one rainy month.
